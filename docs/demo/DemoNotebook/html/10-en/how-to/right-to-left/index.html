Arabic, Hebrew, Persian and Urdu read from the right. NoteCraft draws them that way: the letters join, a line of them hangs from the right edge, and a line that mixes the two directions is laid out by the Unicode algorithm, so a price keeps its number the right way round:

السعر 123 ريال

The fonts that make this possible ship with the app, so the line above looks the same on a machine with no Arabic font installed. Hebrew and Hindi have their own bundled fonts too:

שלום עולם

नमस्ते दुनिया

Three directions, three controls

ControlWhereWhat it sets
PageHome, then DirectionWhich way the whole page reads. A line with no letters of its own follows it
ParagraphThe alignment menu in Basic TextWhich way the paragraph the caret is in reads
RunMore... in the Direction groupWhich way the selection reads, or what you type next

Page direction is NoteCraft's own; the apps people come from have no such setting, and a notebook written in Arabic wants one.

Where the setting is kept

In the text itself. A page direction is dir: rtl in the front matter of a Markdown page or a meta tag in an HTML page. A paragraph direction is one invisible character at its start, the Unicode right-to-left or left-to-right mark. Both survive any other editor.

The Arabic, Persian, Urdu and Hebrew groups of this notebook are written right to left throughout. Open one from the start page and compare.
